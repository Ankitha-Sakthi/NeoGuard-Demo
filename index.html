About NeoGuard Prototype

Intended Use

NeoGuard is a student engineering project prototype for a neonatal multi-parameter vital sign monitor designed for resource-limited, offline-capable environments.

The system aims to demonstrate the feasibility of using low-cost sensors (MAX30102 for pulse oximetry, MLX90614 for contactless temperature) connected to a RISC-V processor (Aries v3) to compute a composite severity indicator for neonatal monitoring.

This prototype runs on simulated data and is intended solely for academic demonstration and engineering evaluation.

⚠ Limitations

  • Not a medical device. This prototype has not undergone any regulatory review, certification, or approval process.
  • Not clinically validated. All thresholds, weights, and severity cut-offs are placeholder values chosen for demonstration. They are not based on clinical evidence.
  • Simulated data only. The dashboard currently displays simulated vital signs. The serial connection feature is experimental.
  • ML model not validated. The risk estimation model was trained on public datasets (PreMo, very-low-birth-weight NICU data) and has not been validated against signals from the MAX30102 sensor or any clinical cohort.
  • Sensor limitations. Consumer-grade sensors like the MAX30102 are not equivalent to medical-grade pulse oximeters. Motion artifacts significantly degrade signal quality.
  • Temperature sensor pending. The MLX90614 infrared temperature sensor has not yet been integrated.
  • No alarm validation. Alert thresholds are placeholders and have not been validated for sensitivity or specificity.

Ethics & Clinical Responsibility

Before this system could be used in any capacity with real patients, the following would be required:

  • Clinical validation studies with appropriate patient populations
  • Ethics committee / IRB approval for any studies involving patients
  • Regulatory approval (e.g. CE marking, FDA clearance) as a medical device
  • Threshold calibration with qualified neonatologists and clinical evidence
  • Extensive reliability and safety testing
  • Proper biomedical engineering oversight and quality management systems

The severity score indicates a potentially concerning pattern based on demo thresholds. It is not a diagnosis and must never be used as the sole basis for clinical decisions.
